Exploring Computer Vision Beyond Object Detection

Lessons from working with video data, custom YOLO datasets, real-time object detection and semantic segmentation.

By Mariam FatimaAugust 20267 min read
CV
In this articleStarting with video dataPreparing datasetsDetection and segmentationThinking about real-time use

Starting with video data

During my machine learning and computer vision internship at IIIT Hyderabad, I worked on pipelines involving raw video, annotation, training and inference. Working with video made it clear that a practical vision system starts well before model training.

Preparing datasets

The workflow included preparing custom YOLO datasets from raw video, annotating data and organizing it for training. Label quality, consistency and coverage are important because they influence how reliably a model learns.

Detection and segmentation

Object detection identifies objects and their locations, while semantic segmentation assigns labels at the pixel level. Working with both tasks helped me understand how the problem definition shapes the pipeline and its evaluation.

Thinking about real-time use

A deployed computer vision system involves more than a trained model. Video processing, inference speed, hardware constraints and the way results are presented all affect its usefulness. The internship also involved developing a fitness application combining computer vision with an interactive, game-like experience.
